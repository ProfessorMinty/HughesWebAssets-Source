## Decisions that affect the exhibit

- Mycelium is a fungal network, not a separate family beside mushrooms, moulds and yeasts.
  The three examples shown are an introduction, not formal taxonomic divisions.
- Mycelium is not restricted to soil. Wood is another explicitly named habitat.
- The diagram is a generic gilled mushroom; it cannot identify the class kit or a wild specimen.
- The life cycle is simplified and conditional. Compatible growth is acknowledged rather
  than implying every solitary spore necessarily produces a mushroom.
- Plant partnerships apply to some fungi. No universal cooperative forest “internet” claim.
- Biological light is a chemical process found in some fungi. It is distinct from electrical
  measurements and from the imagined cyan woodland artwork.
- Electrical activity has evidence; the function and interpretation of particular recordings
  require care. Ion movements support cell processes, but recorded spikes do not establish
  a decoded language. The musical instrument conversion is a human choice.
- The interactive graph contains invented values, not live or recorded fungal data. It has
  no voltage/time units. Pitch mapping is an illustrative musical rule, not a biological claim.
- Safety is taught as a clear rule, never a colour/shape/edibility identification challenge.
