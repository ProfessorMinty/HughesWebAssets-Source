## 1. Real mushroom video — M33

1. Open **Life in motion → Mushroom music → How is real mushroom music made?**
2. Select **Load the creator’s video**, then press the video's own Play button.
3. Confirm the video picture/time advances and its instrument sounds are audible. Try Pause
   and Play, then switch to **Glowing fungi** and confirm it stops.
4. If it stalls, copy the visible status. Try **Try the video again** once, then the creator-page
   link. Report whether the creator-page version plays. A loaded thumbnail is not a playback pass.

The example is a real creator recording driven by conductivity measurements. The local
sixteen-note comparison is invented teaching data and cannot substitute for this check.

## 2. Read-aloud — M41

1. In **Fungi up close**, choose **Reading voice** and an available English voice.
2. Use **Read to me**, then **Stop reading**. Confirm speech starts and stops when requested.
3. Open **Inside a mushroom → 04 · Mycelium** and read that selection. Compare the spoken
   science term with the written pronunciation help. Try Spores and the music explanation too.
4. While reading, change the selected part/activity and then try starting the example music.
   Confirm the old reading stops and does not overlap or describe the former selection.
5. Report any missing voice, error message, awkward pronunciation or hard-to-understand pacing.

Browser voices differ. A voice menu on a separate demo page does not certify this exhibit's
reading or pronunciation. The complete written lesson remains usable without speech.

## 3. Classroom/projector — M46

Use the actual classroom display and a short pupil trial. Observe whether pupils can:

- Find an investigation and identify what to press without a long explanation.
- Explain that a mushroom is part of a fungus; the wider thread network can be hidden.
- Explain that spores can begin new fungal growth when conditions are right.
- Notice that the same example pattern keeps its notes when the instrument changes, and
  that people choose the measurement-to-music rule and sound.

Record prompts pupils needed, text/labels that were hard to see, confusing pictures or
controls, and material misunderstandings. Review the full creator clip before using it in
class. Record teacher/pupil observations in words; do not invent responses or include names.
