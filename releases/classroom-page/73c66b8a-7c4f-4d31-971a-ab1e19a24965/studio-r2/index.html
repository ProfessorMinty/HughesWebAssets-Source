Turn through a lunar month

Moonlight is sunlight bouncing off the Moon. As the Moon travels around Earth, we see different amounts of its lit half. From one new Moon to the next takes about 29½ days.

Moon phases · NASA Science
Follow the Moon through eight phases

Choose a stage. Compare its bright edge with the stage before it.

New Moon

The sunlit half faces away from Earth. The Moon is usually invisible to us at this stage.

Waxing crescent

A bright sliver appears. Over the next few nights we see more of the sunlit half.

First quarter

About a quarter of the cycle has passed. The bright area fills half the disk we see.

Waxing gibbous

More than half the disk looks bright, and the bright part is still growing.

Full Moon

Earth faces nearly the entire sunlit half. A full Moon rises around sunset.

Waning gibbous

More than half the disk still looks bright. Now the bright part grows smaller each night.

Third quarter

Half of the disk looks bright again. Compare the bright side with first quarter.

Waning crescent

Only a thin sliver looks bright before the next new Moon.

Look from two directions

Six words for a Moon watcher Orbit — a path around another object. Phase — the shape of the bright part we see. Crescent — a curved sliver. Gibbous — more than half of the disk looks bright. Waxing — the bright part is growing. Waning — the bright part is shrinking.
